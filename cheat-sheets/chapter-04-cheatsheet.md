## Chapter 4 Cheat Sheet: Responsible AI Governance & Regulatory Awareness

### Responsible AI Pillars (Governance Lens)
| Pillar | Governance Concern |
|---|---|
| Fairness | Avoiding discriminatory/biased outcomes across groups |
| Reliability & Safety | Consistent, safe operation; failure handling |
| Privacy & Security | Data protection, executive risk exposure |
| Inclusiveness | Designing for diverse users/needs |
| Transparency | Explainability, disclosure of AI use |
| Accountability | Clear ownership of AI decisions/outcomes |

**Exam framing:** Each pillar = leadership/governance responsibility, not just a technical checkbox.

### EU AI Act Risk Tiers
| Tier | Examples | Obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable | Social scoring, manipulative/subliminal techniques, exploiting vulnerabilities | Banned outright |
| High risk | Employment/hiring, credit scoring, critical infrastructure, education, law enforcement, essential services | Human oversight, documentation, risk management |
| Limited risk | Chatbots, AI-generated content | Disclosure/transparency obligation only |
| Minimal risk | Spam filters, AI in games | No specific obligation |

**Key dates:** Act in force Aug 2024 → prohibitions applicable Feb 2025 → GPAI model obligations Aug 2025 → most high-risk system obligations Aug 2026.

### Policy Guardrails vs Technical Controls
| Type | Examples | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Policy guardrail | Mandatory human review before publishing AI output; disclosure requirements; approved use-case list | Leadership/legal/compliance |
| Technical control | Content filters, rate limiting, encryption at rest, access controls | Engineering/IT |

**Exam trap:** Relying on technical controls alone = governance gap. Correct answer usually adds a cross-functional policy layer.

### Governance Structures
- Cross-functional AI governance board (legal + compliance + business + technical)
- Named accountable owner per AI use case
- Regular risk review cadence tied to regulatory changes

### Data Privacy/Security as Executive Risk
- Framed as **strategic/reputational risk**, not just an IT configuration issue
- Breaches trigger regulatory, legal, and trust consequences at the organizational level

### Transparency & Accountability Practices
- Disclose AI use to customers and employees (purpose, scope, decision impact)
- Never deploy monitoring/decision AI silently
- Assign accountable owner for every AI-driven decision process

### Setting Stakeholder Expectations
- Generative AI **will** hallucinate and **can** reflect bias — never promise elimination
- Leadership communicates limitations proactively; human review remains part of the process
- Exam trap: 'wait for a better model' or 'hide limitations' are always wrong answers

### Quick Rule of Thumb
If a question asks 'who is responsible' → **leadership/cross-functional governance**, not engineering alone.

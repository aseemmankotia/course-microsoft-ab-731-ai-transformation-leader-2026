## Chapter 1: AI Transformation Opportunities Across Business Functions — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Automation vs Augmentation vs Transformation — key difference? | Automation = AI executes the task with no required human review. Augmentation = AI assists a human who still reviews/edits output. Transformation = redesign of the process or business model itself, not just a task. Exam tip: classify by WHO does final work and WHAT actually changed. |
| 2 | Three signals of AI-readiness in a function? | 1) Repetitive knowledge work 2) Sufficient quality data availability 3) Strong stakeholder appetite/sponsorship. Exam tip: all three must be present — missing even one is a hype-risk flag. |
| 3 | Sales function — dominant generative AI opportunity pattern? | Prospect research acceleration + personalized outreach drafting. Exam tip: NOT autonomous deal-closing — relationship/trust work stays human. |
| 4 | Customer service function — dominant generative AI opportunity pattern? | Ticket deflection for high-volume, low-complexity FAQ-type requests (password resets, order status). Exam tip: complex escalations stay human; full replacement is an overreach distractor. |
| 5 | HR function — genuine transformation vs hype-driven pilot, how to tell? | Genuine: clear internal pain point + sponsor + measurable process gap. Hype: flashy feature with no defined problem or sponsor. Exam tip: absence of a defined pain point is the #1 hype signal. |
| 6 | Finance function — dominant generative AI opportunity pattern? | Drafting/summarizing variance commentary and narrative explanations from already-reconciled data. Exam tip: gen AI drafts the narrative; it does not perform the reconciliation itself. |
| 7 | Operations function — dominant generative AI opportunity pattern? | Summarizing unstructured incident reports into structured SOP documentation. Exam tip: distinguish this from predictive maintenance, a separate non-generative capability. |
| 8 | Marketing function — dominant generative AI opportunity pattern? | Rapid drafting of ad copy/content variations for human review before publishing. Exam tip: this is augmentation (human edits) — not automation (no review) or transformation (business model change). |
| 9 | 'AI for AI's sake' project — defining trait? | A pilot launched because AI is trendy, not because it maps to a validated business pain point or measurable ROI. Exam tip: scenarios test whether you spot missing pain-point/sponsorship, not the underlying tech. |
| 10 | Leadership vocabulary: 'opportunity mapping' vs 'use case'? | Opportunity mapping = the broader exercise of matching function pain points to gen AI capability categories (drafting, summarization, research). Use case = one specific, scoped instance of that mapping. Exam tip: strategic framing favors opportunity mapping language. |
| 11 | Core generative AI capability categories tested for pain-point mapping? | Drafting, summarization, research/synthesis. Exam tip: match the function's stated pain point to one of these three — not to unrelated categories like image generation or predictive analytics. |
| 12 | Cross-functional AI opportunity landscape — which 6 functions are tested? | Sales, Marketing, HR, Finance, Operations, Customer Service. Exam tip: each function has ONE dominant genuine-transformation pattern the exam repeatedly tests — know all six cold. |

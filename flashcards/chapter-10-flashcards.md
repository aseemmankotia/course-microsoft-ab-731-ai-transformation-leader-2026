## Chapter 10: Standing Up a Copilot Center of Excellence and Governance Guardrails — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the two main CoE governance models tested on the exam? | Centralized (single team owns approvals/oversight org-wide) and Federated (central guardrails + business-unit-level champions/approvals). Exam tip: regulated industries lean centralized; large diverse orgs needing speed lean federated. |
| 2 | When does a centralized CoE model fit best? | When consistent, auditable oversight across the whole org is required (e.g., regulated industries like healthcare/finance). Exam tip: watch for 'strict audit requirements across all departments' as the trigger phrase. |
| 3 | When does a federated CoE model fit best? | Large orgs with diverse business units and risk profiles, where central bottlenecks slow adoption. Central team keeps policy guardrails; business units run local reviews. Exam tip: watch for 'delays/bottleneck' + 'many business units' as the trigger. |
| 4 | What is an 'approved-use list' in Copilot governance? | A documented list of vetted agents, permitted use cases, and prohibited data types employees may/may not use. Exam tip: it's the standard governance artifact — verbal-only approval is a wrong answer. |
| 5 | Who on the CoE owns data-handling and regulatory compliance oversight? | The Compliance/Legal representative. Exam tip: don't confuse with HR (people impact) or business unit leads (functional priorities). |
| 6 | What must happen before a new third-party agent touching PII is scaled org-wide? | CoE security/data-handling review and vetting against the approved-use list. Exam tip: 'deploy immediately for productivity' is always the wrong-answer trap. |
| 7 | Name two metrics used to measure CoE effectiveness. | Time-to-approval for new use cases, and rate of policy/compliance violations post-rollout. Exam tip: license counts and marketing spend are distractors, not governance metrics. |
| 8 | What is the core purpose of a Copilot CoE? | A coordinating structure that governs, enables, and safely scales Copilot adoption across the org. Exam tip: it is NOT a helpdesk and NOT the team writing every prompt. |
| 9 | What are typical roles represented on a Copilot CoE? | IT, Security, Compliance/Legal, HR, and Business Unit leads. Exam tip: a question naming only one function (e.g., only Marketing) as 'the CoE' is a trap — CoE is cross-functional. |
| 10 | What risk arises from eliminating central guardrails and letting each department self-govern Copilot? | Inconsistent security/data-handling practices and reduced ability to detect or contain violations. Exam tip: 'no shared policy, faster adoption, no downside' answer choices are wrong-answer traps. |
| 11 | Does Microsoft provide an org's internal Copilot governance/approval process? | No — governance, guardrails, and approvals are the customer's responsibility; Microsoft provides the platform and admin controls, not the org's CoE function. Exam tip: 'route governance through Microsoft support' is always wrong. |
| 12 | What guardrail categories does a mature CoE typically define? | Usage policies, approved-use lists, and data-handling rules. Exam tip: these three categories map directly to the 'Policy guardrails' heavily-tested concept — expect scenario questions naming one and asking which category it belongs to. |

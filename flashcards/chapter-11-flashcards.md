## Chapter 11: Process Redesign, Adoption Metrics, and Scaling from Pilot to Enterprise — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Adoption metric — definition and 2 examples | Measures whether people are USING the tool. Examples: active usage rate, license utilization. Exam tip: high adoption ≠ proof of business value. |
| 2 | Impact metric — definition and 2 examples | Measures whether the BUSINESS PROCESS improved. Examples: cycle-time reduction, cost savings, quality/error-rate improvement. Exam tip: this is what proves ROI, not usage. |
| 3 | Why is high license utilization alone NOT sufficient to justify scaling? | It only proves engagement, not that the process got faster/cheaper/better. Scaling decisions require impact metrics improving relative to baseline too. |
| 4 | Process mapping classification: 3 categories to tag each process step | Augment (agent assists human judgment), Remove (agent fully replaces the step), Unchanged (requires human judgment/exception handling). Exam tests applying this classification to a scenario. |
| 5 | When must baseline metrics be captured? | BEFORE the pilot begins. Capture the same metrics (cycle time, cost, quality, volume) that will be re-measured post-pilot — otherwise improvement can't be proven. |
| 6 | Iteration loop — correct order of 4 stages | Pilot → gather feedback → redesign process → re-pilot to validate → (then decide to) scale. Exam trap: choices that skip re-piloting after redesign. |
| 7 | Two named common scaling failure modes | 1) Under-resourced support (no clear help path when the agent errs). 2) No reinforcement (no ongoing manager/process reminders to keep using redesigned workflow). Both cause usage collapse after a successful pilot. |
| 8 | Scaling readiness — what should be true before going enterprise-wide? | Both adoption AND impact metrics show sustained improvement vs. baseline across a re-pilot, not just a single successful trial. Exam trap: scaling on adoption alone or a single pilot cycle. |
| 9 | What should an executive sponsor report primarily contain? | Adoption vs. impact metrics compared to the pre-pilot baseline, plus a clear next-step recommendation (continue/redesign/scale) — not raw logs or feature lists. |
| 10 | Exam trap: pilot shows high usage but no cycle-time improvement — what's the correct next step? | Do NOT scale. Re-map the process to find where the tool isn't removing/augmenting real work, redesign, and re-pilot before considering scale. |
| 11 | Cost savings vs. cycle-time reduction — same category or different? | Same category — both are IMPACT metrics (business outcome measures), as opposed to adoption metrics like active users. |
| 12 | Quality metric in adoption/impact framework — where does it belong? | Impact metric. Quality (e.g., error rate, accuracy of output) reflects whether the process outcome improved, same bucket as cost savings and cycle time. |

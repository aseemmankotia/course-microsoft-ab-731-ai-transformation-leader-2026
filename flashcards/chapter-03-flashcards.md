## Chapter 3: Building the Business Case: ROI and AI Strategy Alignment — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the four required components of a complete Gen AI business case? | Cost, Benefit, Risk, Timeline. Exam tip: a business case missing any one of these four is considered incomplete, even if cost/benefit data looks strong. |
| 2 | Hard ROI benefit vs. soft/qualitative benefit — give one example of each. | Hard: reduction in handle time (seconds), cost savings ($). Soft: employee satisfaction, customer sentiment, confidence scores. Exam tip: soft benefits should be labeled separately, not folded into dollar totals. |
| 3 | What is the most common ROI measurement pitfall tested on AB-731? | Measuring adoption/usage (logins, prompts sent, licenses activated) instead of measuring actual business impact (cost, time, quality outcomes). Exam tip: high adoption with flat impact metrics = value not yet proven. |
| 4 | What five categories make up a complete Gen AI TCO analysis? | 1) Licensing 2) Compute/API usage costs 3) Integration & change management 4) Ongoing governance/compliance monitoring 5) Training. Exam tip: licensing alone is never a complete TCO answer. |
| 5 | How should an AI initiative metric be chosen to align with an existing corporate OKR? | Pick the AI-driven metric that directly maps to the OKR's stated outcome (e.g., OKR = reduce resolution time → metric = AI-assisted resolution time reduction vs. baseline). Exam tip: license counts and training completion do NOT count as OKR-aligned outcome metrics. |
| 6 | What are the three core elements of setting realistic expectations when pitching Gen AI to leadership? | 1) Present a range of outcomes based on pilot data (not vendor best-case marketing) 2) Explicitly state known model limitations 3) Define what human oversight remains in place. Exam tip: omitting limitations = classic overselling trap. |
| 7 | Why is 'adoption rate' a risky primary success metric for a Gen AI initiative? | Adoption measures usage, not outcome. High adoption can occur while cost, time, or quality metrics stay flat — meaning no proven business value yet. Exam tip: always pair adoption metrics with impact metrics. |
| 8 | What should you do when a pilot shows high adoption but no measurable impact on KPIs? | Pause/delay full-scale expansion until impact metrics (cost, time, quality) show measurable improvement — do not expand based on adoption alone. Exam tip: this is a heavily tested scenario question pattern. |
| 9 | In executive communication, what is the risk of citing only vendor benchmark/marketing figures in a business case? | Sets unrealistic expectations disconnected from your organization's actual pilot data, eroding stakeholder trust when real-world results underperform vendor claims. Exam tip: always ground projections in your own pilot data. |
| 10 | What belongs in the 'Risk' section of a Gen AI business case? | Model accuracy/hallucination risk, data privacy exposure, governance/compliance gaps, and change management risk (workforce resistance/skills gaps). Exam tip: cost/benefit-only business cases are incomplete without this section. |
| 11 | Give one example of change management cost often omitted from Gen AI TCO estimates. | Employee retraining time, workflow redesign, and internal support/helpdesk resourcing during rollout. Exam tip: 'licensing only' TCO answers are a distractor trap. |
| 12 | What is the key difference between measuring 'usage' and measuring 'impact' in an AI ROI framework? | Usage = how much the tool is being used (logins, prompts, licenses active). Impact = the resulting business outcome change (cost, time, quality, revenue). Exam tip: correct ROI reporting always centers on impact metrics. |

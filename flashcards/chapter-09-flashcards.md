## Chapter 9: Assessing Readiness and Leading Change Management — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the 5 readiness dimensions assessed before an AI rollout? | Data, culture, skills, leadership sponsorship, infrastructure/licensing. Exam tip: a scenario with strong tech but stalled adoption usually points to a culture or sponsorship gap, not a technical one. |
| 2 | What are the 5 stages of the ADKAR change management model, in order? | Awareness → Desire → Knowledge → Ability → Reinforcement. Exam tip: 'trained but not using it' = Ability gap; 'trained and using it, then reverting' = Reinforcement gap. |
| 3 | Pilot rollout vs. enterprise-wide rollout — when to use each? | Pilot: validate use case and surface risk with minimal exposure, small controlled group. Enterprise-wide: only after pilot + department scale-up prove repeatability. Exam tip: never skip straight to enterprise scale. |
| 4 | Champions network vs. centralized help desk — which supports peer reinforcement? | Champions network: embedded peer power-users who model behavior and give in-team support. Help desk: handles technical tickets, not culture/trust reinforcement. Exam tip: champions networks target the 'reinforcement' ADKAR stage. |
| 5 | Correct rollout sequencing pattern for enterprise AI adoption | Pilot → single department → additional departments → enterprise-wide. Exam tip: each stage validates and de-risks the next; skipping stages is a common wrong-answer trap. |
| 6 | What role does active executive sponsorship play in adoption velocity? | It's the top driver of adoption success (visible modeling, communication, reinforcement) — not just approval. Exam tip: 'manager approved but never mentions it' = passive, not active, sponsorship = root cause of stalled adoption. |
| 7 | Resistance pattern: 'This will replace my job.' What does this signal and what mitigates it? | Signals a Desire-stage gap driven by fear. Mitigate with transparent communication about role evolution (not elimination), leadership reassurance, and involving employees in shaping the tool's use. |
| 8 | Resistance pattern: 'I don't trust the AI's output.' What ADKAR stage and mitigation apply? | Ability-stage gap. Mitigate with supervised practice, output verification workflows, and visible examples of correct/incorrect output handling — not just more training content. |
| 9 | What communication cadence pattern prevents awareness decay during a rollout? | Recurring, multi-channel updates (regular check-ins, success stories, FAQs) throughout the rollout — not a single kickoff message. Exam tip: 'one email at launch, survey 6 months later' is a designed-to-fail scenario. |
| 10 | Role-based training vs. one-size-fits-all training — which supports skilling program design? | Role-based training tailors content/depth to how each role actually uses the tool, improving Knowledge→Ability transfer. One-size-fits-all often leaves gaps for non-technical roles. |
| 11 | Why does 100% training completion not guarantee adoption? | Training completion confirms Knowledge only. Ability (confidence/trust to apply it) and Desire (motivation) are separate ADKAR stages that can remain unmet even with full training completion. |
| 12 | How does infrastructure/licensing readiness show up as a rollout blocker? | Manifests as access errors, seat shortages, or feature gating — distinct from culture/skill resistance. Exam tip: differentiate 'can't use it' (infra/licensing) from 'won't use it' (culture/desire). |

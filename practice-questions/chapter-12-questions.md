**Q1.** You are 20 minutes into the AB-731 exam and have answered 15 of 45 questions. Question 16 is a dense scenario you don't immediately understand. What should you do?

A) Flag it, submit your best guess, and move on immediately
B) Keep working the question until you solve it, since skipping wastes time
C) Leave it blank and continue to question 17 without answering
D) Restart the exam timer by requesting a break

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

The flag-and-return technique means you always lock in a best guess before moving on — this protects your score if time runs out, and you can revisit flagged items in the final review window.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Sinking unlimited time into one question at a ~1-question-per-minute pace guarantees you'll run out of time for later, easier questions
- Leaving it blank scores zero with certainty; a guessed answer has a nonzero chance of being correct
- There is no mechanism to restart the timer via a break on this exam format

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** With 10 minutes left and all 45 questions attempted, you have time to fully re-check only 2 of 3 domains: Domain 1 (35-40% weight), Domain 2 (30-35% weight), Domain 3 (25-30% weight). Which domains should you prioritize reviewing?

A) Domain 3 and Domain 2, since lower-weighted domains are easier to fix quickly
B) Only Domain 1, spending all 10 minutes there
C) Split evenly across all three domains regardless of weight
D) Domain 1 and Domain 2, since they carry the two highest weight ranges

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

Review time should scale with published domain weight. Domain 1 and Domain 2 together account for 65-75% of the exam, so correcting mistakes there has the highest expected point impact.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Prioritizing the lowest-weighted domain wastes limited review time on the section that matters least
- Ignoring Domain 2 entirely skips the second-highest weighted section
- Even splitting ignores that weight ranges are not equal, so it under-reviews the heaviest domains

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q3.** At 45 questions in 45 minutes, roughly how much time should you budget per question to leave a buffer for final review?

A) About 2 minutes per question, planning to finish with no time left
B) About 30 seconds per question, leaving 25+ minutes for review
C) About 55-60 seconds per question, leaving roughly 5-8 minutes for final review
D) No fixed pace — answer as slowly as needed since there is no time pressure

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

A 1-question-per-minute pace on a 45-question/45-minute exam naturally reserves a small buffer if you move slightly faster than one minute on straightforward items, giving 5-8 minutes for a final weighted review pass.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- A 2-minute pace leaves zero buffer and risks not finishing
- 30 seconds per question is unrealistic for scenario-based items and would sacrifice accuracy
- Ignoring pacing entirely is the primary cause of unfinished exams

</details>

- Informal team preference without documented accountability fails governance requirements

**Q5.** A question describes two correct actions needed to resolve an incident but is phrased as a single-answer item. Which single option is the BEST response?

A) Only the first action, since only one option can be selected
B) Only the second action, since it addresses the immediate symptom
C) Skip the question since it seems to require two answers
D) The bundled option combining both actions into one answer choice

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

AB-731 uses single-best-answer format exclusively. When two actions are both required, the correct option bundles them together (e.g., 'Do X AND review Y') rather than presenting them as separate choices.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Choosing only the first action ignores half of the required remediation
- Choosing only the second action treats a symptom without addressing the root cause
- Skipping guarantees zero credit when a bundled correct option exists

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

**Q8.** A question asks you to choose the BEST tool from a comparison matrix based on a scenario with cost, scalability, and compliance constraints. What is the correct strategy for this question type?

A) Pick the most feature-rich tool regardless of the stated constraints
B) Pick the newest tool mentioned, assuming exams favor recent releases
C) Pick the option listed first, since exam answer keys are alphabetical
D) Eliminate options that violate any stated hard constraint first, then compare remaining options against the scenario's priority

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

The tool-selection matrix approach requires eliminating any option that fails a hard requirement (e.g., compliance) before weighing the remaining choices against the scenario's stated priority, such as cost or scale.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Extra features are irrelevant if the tool fails a stated hard constraint
- Recency is not a tested criterion and is a common distractor pattern
- Answer position has no correlation with correctness on this exam

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

## Chapter 3: Building the Business Case: ROI and AI Strategy Alignment — Practice Questions

**Q1.** A retail company's Gen AI chatbot pilot shows 85% employee adoption within 60 days, but customer satisfaction scores and average handle time are unchanged. Leadership wants to expand the pilot company-wide based on the adoption number alone. What is the BEST response from the AI program lead?

A) Approve expansion since 85% adoption exceeds the 70% threshold typically used for enterprise rollouts
B) Recommend expansion but reduce the reporting cadence so leadership sees fewer negative signals
C) Recommend pausing expansion until impact metrics (handle time, CSAT, cost per ticket) show measurable improvement, since adoption measures usage, not value
D) Recommend expansion and add adoption bonuses for teams to push the number to 100%

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Adoption is a leading/usage indicator, not a business outcome. The exam tests recognizing that high adoption with flat impact metrics means the tool isn't driving value yet — expansion should wait for impact evidence.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- There is no universal '70% adoption threshold' in the discipline; using adoption as a proxy for value is the exact pitfall being tested
- Reducing reporting visibility hides risk from stakeholders and violates transparent executive communication practice
- Pushing adoption higher without addressing impact just amplifies the measurement pitfall, it doesn't fix it

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q2.** A CFO asks for the total cost of ownership (TCO) of a new Gen AI content-generation tool before approving budget. Which set of cost categories BEST reflects a complete TCO analysis?

A) Software licensing fees only, since that is the only recurring cost
B) Licensing, compute/API usage costs, integration and change management, ongoing governance/compliance monitoring, and training
C) One-time implementation cost and employee headcount reduction estimate
D) Licensing and marketing costs to promote the new tool internally

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

TCO for Gen AI initiatives must include licensing, variable usage/compute costs, change management, governance/compliance overhead, and training — not just the sticker price of the license.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Ignores usage-based compute costs and change management, both major hidden costs in Gen AI TCO
- Headcount reduction is a benefit estimate, not a cost, and this option omits ongoing operational costs
- Marketing spend is not a standard TCO component and this option omits compute, governance, and training costs

</details>

**Q3.** During an executive pitch for a Gen AI initiative, which communication approach BEST sets realistic expectations while still building support?

A) Emphasize only best-case benchmark results from vendor marketing materials to maximize excitement
B) Avoid mentioning limitations so leadership doesn't lose confidence in the initiative
C) Present a range of expected outcomes tied to a pilot's measured data, explicitly state known model limitations, and define what human oversight will remain in place
D) Promise full automation of the target process within the first quarter to secure faster approval

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

Effective executive communication pairs a data-backed benefit range with explicit disclosure of model limits and retained human oversight — this avoids overselling while maintaining credibility and support.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Relying on vendor marketing best cases rather than measured pilot data sets unrealistic expectations that later erode trust
- Omitting limitations is a classic overselling trap that leads to disillusionment when the model underperforms
- Promising full automation on an aggressive timeline ignores realistic model capability and typically fails, damaging future AI initiative credibility

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q4.** A company's existing corporate OKR is 'Reduce average customer support resolution time by 20% this fiscal year.' Which Gen AI initiative metric BEST aligns the AI program to this existing OKR?

A) Reduction in average resolution time for tickets handled with Gen AI assistance, compared to the baseline
B) Number of Gen AI licenses purchased across the support organization
C) Percentage of support agents who completed Gen AI training
D) Number of prompts submitted to the Gen AI tool per week

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: A**

Aligning AI strategy to KPIs/OKRs means selecting a metric that directly maps to the existing organizational goal — here, resolution time reduction directly mirrors the stated OKR.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- License count is a cost/adoption input, not tied to the resolution-time outcome
- Training completion is a readiness metric, not an outcome metric aligned to the OKR
- Prompt volume measures usage/adoption, not the business outcome the OKR targets

</details>

**Q5.** Which of the following is an example of a soft (qualitative) benefit in a Gen AI business case, as distinct from a hard ROI benefit?

A) A 15% reduction in average call handle time measured in seconds
B) Improved employee satisfaction scores reported in a post-pilot survey
C) A $200,000 annual reduction in outsourced content production costs
D) A 30% decrease in ticket escalations tracked in the support system

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

Soft/qualitative benefits are difficult to directly monetize and are typically captured via surveys or sentiment (e.g., satisfaction), unlike hard ROI benefits which are measured in time, cost, or currency.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Handle time reduction is a directly measurable, quantifiable hard benefit
- A dollar-denominated cost reduction is a textbook hard ROI benefit
- Escalation rate reduction is a quantifiable operational metric, making it a hard benefit

</details>

**Q6.** A business case for a Gen AI initiative is being reviewed by the finance committee. Which structural component is MISSING if the document only includes projected cost savings and a rollout timeline?

A) A restatement of the vendor's product marketing description
B) A list of every employee who will use the tool
C) A risk assessment covering model accuracy, data privacy, and change management risks
D) The company's mission statement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: C**

A complete business case requires cost, benefit, risk, AND timeline. Omitting risk (accuracy, privacy, governance, change management exposure) leaves the case incomplete and unable to support informed decision-making.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Marketing descriptions are not a required business case component and add no decision-relevant information
- A full user list is an implementation detail, not a core business case structural element
- A mission statement is organizational context, not a required component of the cost/benefit/risk/timeline structure

</details>

**Q7.** A mid-size insurance firm wants to select an approach to justify a Gen AI underwriting-assistant investment to its board. Which approach BEST balances hard ROI and soft benefits in the business case?

A) Present only the projected reduction in underwriting cycle time in hours and dollars, omitting any employee or customer experience data
B) Present projected cycle-time and cost reductions (hard ROI) alongside employee time-savings and improved underwriter confidence scores (soft benefits), each clearly labeled
C) Present only employee satisfaction survey results since hard financial projections could be seen as overpromising
D) Combine all benefits into a single dollar figure without separating hard and soft components

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: B**

A strong business case clearly separates and labels hard ROI (measurable financial/time impact) from soft benefits (qualitative improvements), giving the board a transparent, defensible view of both value types.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Omitting soft benefits understates the full value proposition and ignores stakeholder experience impact
- Relying only on soft/qualitative data avoids quantifying financial impact, which finance committees require
- Blending hard and soft benefits into one number obscures measurement rigor and undermines credibility with a financially literate audience

> ⚠️ Frequently missed on the exam — read the options carefully.

</details>

**Q8.** Which of the following is the MOST common pitfall organizations make when measuring the ROI of a generative AI initiative?

A) Tracking too few financial metrics
B) Including risk assessments in the business case
C) Setting a timeline for expected benefits realization
D) Measuring adoption/usage rates as a proxy for business impact instead of measuring actual outcome metrics

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Correct: D**

The most frequently cited ROI measurement pitfall is conflating adoption/usage metrics (logins, prompts, licenses used) with actual business impact (cost savings, cycle time, quality improvements).

**Why the others are wrong:**
- Tracking too few financial metrics is a possible issue but not the specifically-named common pitfall tested
- Including risk assessments strengthens a business case; it is not a pitfall
- Setting a realization timeline is a best practice, not a measurement pitfall

</details>
